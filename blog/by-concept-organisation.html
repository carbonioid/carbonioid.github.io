by-concept organisation

Organising information in Obsidian is difficult. A lot of time, I feel like what I write down isn't focused properly by where I'm writing it, and I'm never able to reference what I've written before. Specifically, harder-to-categorise thoughts and information simply get lost.

Notes need to give the right level of specificity so that information is focused and contextualised, but not grouped with too much information to be undiscoverable.

I've been struggling with this in my own vault for a while; here are some of the things that didn't really work:

  • Massive notes: have one single note for "Social Thoughts" or "Obsidian" and put all the information there. These files are so big you can never find anything, and the lack of specificity means what you write down is often vague and unstructured.
  • Single notes: have a single note for each piece of information; give it some tags to link it to broader topics. In this case, you end up organising thoughts with long queried lists of everything with a given tag, which isn't much better than having one big note in the first place (as these tags tend to be very broad.)

By-concept Organisation

My fairly new solution is By-concept Organisation. Each piece of info should be associated to one reasonably specific concept (e.g. a book) and go in that respective note. This approach:

  • Enforces structure on what you write down because it has to fit within the chosen note
  • Keeps information discoverable, as the scope of these notes is kept small but not too small

The key is to keep the scope of these concepts roughly the correct size. The approach is different for factual information and less structured thoughts, so here's both.

Factual information

Factual information is specific (often atomic) pieces of information about something. For me, this is already reasonably well organised using knowlege notes but note names were often still too vague.

We store factual information by specific Proper Nouns. These notes are then grouped by type to keep them searchable. For example, I have a template and base system for both media notes and software notes. Media notes are any kind of media I consume: book, video, lecture etc; and software notes are for any piece of software I use: desktop apps, websites, CLIs.

A software note contains all the information about troubleshooting that software, possible alternatives to it, and if it becomes too large it can become a router to other notes about the software (see this post for info on what a router note is.)

A media note normally contains a description of the media, a rating, some metadata, and any thoughts that it inspired. Also, keeping track of all your media in a big list is just really fun and motivates me to consume more quality stuff like good books.

This can be extended to any concept which you can attach information to. The utility of linking ideas to a specific thing is that the correct scope comes for free.

Thoughts

Thoughts are the more fickle part of the puzzle: these are the less focused thoughts you have about mental health, people you know, productivity, how you think and so on. They aren't as easily categorisable and that's what makes it difficult. Factual information isn't really too hard; the fact I've come up with a good system for this is what I'm excited about.

When we were organising facts we used proper nouns. Well, you could try to do that here but the nouns are all too broad to be helpful: "Social Anxiety", "Identity", "Hobbies." What is one to do? Invent your own nouns.

Basically, for any recurring concept you're thinking about, make up a name for it and make the note. Some things might already have a name (for example, people) but other more vague trains of thoughts will need structure imposed on them.

If you're careful and make these names specific enough, you've invented structure which keeps your written thoughts searchable and specific. Additionally, in my experience having a name for a group of thoughts you're having helps to investigate them more deeply.

I call these "dialect notes", in that you're adding new words to a personal dialect. It's similar to the concept of Evergreen notes. As you would with evergreen notes, as much as dialect notes exist to be written in they also exist to be referenced.

list of my current dialect notes (in an obsidian base)

If you're writing about thoughts somewhere else (maybe in a diary), you can link to the relevant dialect notes. This helps mentally include all the context from these ideas without restating them. Also, I tend to keep thoughts inspired by media in media notes, so referencing dialect notes is very important there.

A brief example: I have a note I'm working on currently called "Decoupling." This is the idea of decoupling your own identity from the societal expectations of your sex, gender, age etc and being more able to choose who you are. The first paragraph reads:

"Decoupling is the process of decoupling my thoughts and (specifically) my actions from the expectations that society as a whole, and people as individuals have of me, so that I can simply be who I wish to be."

I've linked to Decoupling in diary entries (which I keep on Obsidian) and when writing thoughts about media.

Conclusion

To recap, the properties I was going for in these notes was that they enforced structure on what I wrote, and had the scope that brings in the correct amount of context while keeping the note searchable and concise. This is the first time I've found a solution where I feel like writing down actually helps me synthesise and organise rather than those words just disappearing into the void, so I thought I'd share it in case it helps anyone else.

You might want to have broader or narrower notes, or name them differently, but I think the idea of organising thoughts by specific concepts is definitely worthwhile if you haven't found a solution you're happy with yet.
